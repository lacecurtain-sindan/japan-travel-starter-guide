Narita Airport to Tokyo: which train should you take?

Updated November 2026

Short answerStaying around Ueno or Asakusa? Take the Skyliner. Staying near Tokyo Station or Shinjuku? Take the N'EX. On a tight budget and not in a hurry? Take the Access Express.

The three options at a glance

TrainBest forTimeFare (adult)
SkylinerUeno, Asakusa, NipporiAbout 36 min to Nippori, 41 min to Keisei-Ueno¥2,580 at the station; ¥2,310 online for overseas visitors
Access ExpressBudget, light luggageAround 60 to 75 min to UenoRoughly ¥1,300, no reservation
N'EX (Narita Express)Tokyo Station, Shinjuku, JR Pass holdersAbout 1 hour to Tokyo StationAbout ¥3,000; covered by the JR Pass

Skyliner: the fastest way to Ueno

The Skyliner runs non-stop from Narita to Nippori and Keisei-Ueno. Every seat is reserved and there is space for large suitcases. From Ueno you can reach Asakusa in a few minutes by subway.

The fare is made of a base fare plus a Skyliner ticket. Buying online in advance as an overseas visitor is cheaper than the station counter, and you skip the ticket queue after a long flight.

Book a Skyliner ticket online

Note: a Suica or other IC card can pay the base fare, but you still need a Skyliner ticket for the reserved seat.

Access Express: cheapest, but slower

This Keisei train uses the same fast line for part of the way but stops more often and has no reserved seats. You can tap in with an IC card. It is a good choice if you arrive outside rush hour with light luggage.

N'EX: best for Tokyo Station and Shinjuku

JR's Narita Express runs directly to Tokyo Station and continues to Shinagawa, Shibuya and Shinjuku without changing trains. If you have a JR Pass, the N'EX is included, which usually makes it the obvious pick.

Quick decision guide

  • Hotel in Ueno, Asakusa or nearby: Skyliner
  • Hotel near Tokyo Station, Shinjuku or Shibuya: N'EX
  • Holding a JR Pass: N'EX
  • Saving money matters more than time: Access Express
