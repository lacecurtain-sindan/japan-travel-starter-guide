eSIM vs pocket WiFi vs SIM card: which is best for Japan?

Updated November 2026

Short answerFor most solo travelers and couples with recent phones, an eSIM is the easiest: you set it up before you fly and have data the moment you land. Groups sharing one connection or travelers with older phones may prefer pocket WiFi.

Compare the three options

OptionGood forWatch out for
eSIMRecent phones, solo or couples, no pickup neededYour phone must support eSIM and be carrier-unlocked
Pocket WiFiGroups, several devices, older phonesPick up and return, keep it charged, everyone stays close
Physical SIMPhones without eSIMSwapping cards, keeping your home SIM safe

Before you buy an eSIM

  1. Check that your phone supports eSIM (most iPhones from XS onward and many recent Android phones do).
  2. Check that your phone is unlocked. A phone locked to your home carrier will not accept another eSIM.
  3. Install the eSIM at home over WiFi, then switch it on when you land.

The detail most guides skip: a Japanese phone number

Most travel eSIMs are data-only. That is fine for maps and messaging, but a few bookings in Japan ask for a Japanese phone number. One example: buying Ghibli Museum tickets on a Lawson Loppi machine in a convenience store. If you expect to need one, choose a plan that includes a number, or book those tickets online before your trip.
